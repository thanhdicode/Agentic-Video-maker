# Why Is the Sky Blue?

## Scene 1: Hook
Hey friends, look up at the sky on a sunny day. It is bright blue! But have you ever wondered why?

## Scene 2: Sunlight is white
Here is a fun secret. The light from the Sun looks white, but it is actually made of all the colors of the rainbow mixed together.

## Scene 3: Light travels in waves
Light moves like tiny waves. Some colors have long, slow waves. Other colors have short, bouncy waves.

## Scene 4: Blue is short
Blue light has very short waves. Red light has much longer waves. That difference is the key to our mystery.

## Scene 5: Atmosphere full of particles
Our planet is wrapped in a blanket of air called the atmosphere. It is full of tiny gas particles, smaller than a grain of sand.

## Scene 6: Blue scatters
When sunlight reaches those tiny particles, the short blue waves bounce and scatter in every direction, like a pinball machine.

## Scene 7: Blue everywhere
Because blue light scatters all over the sky, it reaches our eyes from everywhere above us. That is why the sky looks blue.

## Scene 8: Red goes straight
Red and orange light waves are longer and stronger. They mostly pass straight through the air without bouncing around much.

## Scene 9: Sunset path
At sunrise and sunset, the Sun is low in the sky. Sunlight has to travel through a much thicker layer of air to reach us.

## Scene 10: Blue gone
By the time the light reaches your eyes, most of the blue waves have scattered away into the distance, leaving red, orange, and pink light.

## Scene 11: Pollution and dust
Tiny bits of dust, smoke, or pollution can also scatter light and change the color of the sky, making it hazy or extra colorful.

## Scene 12: Deep blue overhead
On a clear day, the sky looks deepest blue right above your head, because you are looking through the shortest amount of air.

## Scene 13: Lighter near horizon
Near the horizon, the sky looks lighter or even whitish. That is because sunlight travels through more air and gets scattered more.

## Scene 14: No atmosphere means black sky
If Earth had no atmosphere, the sky would be black, even during the day. That is exactly what astronauts see on the Moon.

## Scene 15: Astronaut view
On the Moon, the sky is completely black because there is no air to scatter sunlight, even though the Sun is blazing hot and bright.

## Scene 16: Remember this
So remember, the sky is blue because tiny particles in the air scatter short blue light waves more than any other color.

## Scene 17: Sunset recap
And at sunset, the red and orange light reaches your eyes because the blue light has already scattered far away.
